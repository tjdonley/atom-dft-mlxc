\title{Scale-invariant multipole local expansion features and a self-consistent

\author{A.~J.~Medford}

\begin{abstract}
\PH{Abstract handled separately.} We introduce SIMPLE, a complete, orthonormal,
scale-invariant local expansion of the electron density, and use it to build a
self-consistent exchange functional from an explicit model of the exchange hole.
The descriptors reproduce the homogeneous-electron-gas limit by construction,
are exactly rotationally covariant, and remain numerically stable as the density
vanishes. Projecting the Coulomb interaction onto the same orthonormal basis
turns the exchange-energy integral into a closed contraction of monopole
descriptors, yielding a functional that is exact in the homogeneous and
one-electron (Fermi--Amaldi) limits and matches the gradient expansion to fourth
order. Self-consistent atomic calculations reproduce optimized-effective-potential
exchange potentials and Hartree--Fock energies across the first rows of the
periodic table.
\end{abstract}

\section{Theory}

\subsection{SIMPLE feature definitions}
\label{sec:features}

Let $\rho_\sigma$ be the spin-$\sigma$ density. About a point $\rr_0$ we project
the local density onto an orthonormal kernel basis on the ball
$B_{\Rc}=\{\rr:|\rr|\le\Rc\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:projection}
c_{n\ell m}(\rr_0)=\int_{B_{\Rc}} K_{n\ell m}(\rr)\,\rho_\sigma(\rr_0+\rr)\,d^3r .
\end{equation}
The kernel is $K_{n\ell m}(\rr)=R_{n\ell}(r)\,\Ylm(\hat\rr)$, with real spherical
harmonics $\Ylm$ and Dirichlet spherical-Bessel radial
functions $R_{n\ell}(r)=\mathcal N_{n\ell}\,j_\ell(z_{n\ell}r/\Rc)$
($z_{n\ell}$ the positive zeros of $j_\ell$), orthonormal under
$\int_0^{\Rc} r^2 R_{n\ell}R_{n'\ell}\,dr=\delta_{nn'}$. This is the same class of
local density expansion used by atomistic descriptors such as
SOAP~\cite{Bartok2013}, ACE~\cite{Drautz2019}, and SNAP~\cite{Thompson2015}, and
by convolutional density functionals~\cite{Lei2019,Lei2022,Sahoo2024}.

\paragraph*{Local density scale.}
The local scale is the ball-averaged density and its Fermi wavevector,
$\bar\rho_\sigma(R)=\tfrac{3}{R^3}\int_0^R r^2\rho_{\sigma,00}\,dr$ and
$\bar\kF(R)=(6\pi^2\bar\rho_{\sigma,\mathrm{safe}})^{1/3}$, where $\rho_{\sigma,00}$
is the spherical-average ($\ell=0$) profile of the local density and the smooth
floor $\bar\rho_{\sigma,\mathrm{safe}}=(\bar\rho_\sigma^2+\rho_{\min}^2)^{1/2}$
regularizes vanishing density ($\rho_{\min}$ a small density floor).

\subsection{Scale invariance}
\label{sec:scale}

We require features invariant under uniform coordinate scaling
$\rho_\sigma(\rr)\mapsto\lambda^3\rho_\sigma(\lambda\rr)$. Exact invariance demands
a density-adaptive cutoff $\propto\bar\kF^{-1}$, which cannot be a single fixed
window; SIMPLE keeps a fixed window and reconstructs the adaptive-window
descriptors by an exact linear map.

\paragraph*{Adaptive radius.}
Holding the dimensionless cutoff fixed at a target $\xi^*$ defines the adaptive
radius $\Rad$ through
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adaptive}
g(\Rad)=\xi^*,\quad g(R)\equiv R\,\bar\kF(R),\quad \Rad\le\Rc\ \text{(clamped)} .
\end{equation}
No scaling factor is supplied: $\Rad$ is a functional of the density alone and
maps exactly to $\Rad/\lambda$ under the scaling above.

\paragraph*{Dilation identity.}
A change of variables in Eq.~\eqref{eq:projection} gives the exact covariance of
the coefficients at corresponding cutoffs,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dilation}
c_{n\ell m}\bigl[\rho_\lambda;\,R/\lambda\bigr]=\lambda^{3/2}\,
c_{n\ell m}\bigl[\rho;\,R\bigr],
\end{equation}
because the basis depends on $r$ only through the ratio $z_{n\ell}r/R$
(App.~\ref{app:scale}). A uniform scaling thus dilates the radial sampling
wavevectors but loses no information: the descriptors an adaptive cutoff would
produce are recoverable from fixed-window data.

\paragraph*{Mean-split transfer.}
The reconstruction acts on the fluctuation about the window mean --- the constant
maps analytically through the monopole integrals $A_n(R)=\int_{B_R}K_{n00}\,d^3r$,
removing boundary oscillations,
\begin{align}
\label{eq:meansplit}
\tilde c_{n\ell m}&=c_{n\ell m}-\bar\rho_\sigma(\Rc)A_n(\Rc)\,\delta_{\ell0}\delta_{m0},\\
\label{eq:transfer}
c'_{m\ell m'}&=\bar\rho_\sigma(\Rc)A_m(\Rad)\,\delta_{\ell0}\delta_{m'0}\notag\\
&\quad+\sum_{n=0}^{\nin-1}\mathsf M^{(\ell)}_{mn}(\Rad)\,\tilde c_{n\ell m'} ,
\end{align}
with transfer matrices $\mathsf M^{(\ell)}_{mn}(\Rad)=\int_0^{\Rad}r^2
R^{(\Rad)}_{m\ell}R^{(\Rc)}_{n\ell}\,dr$. For a uniform density $\tilde c\equiv0$,
so the HEG limit is exact at any truncation. Surplus inner channels
($\nin>\nout$) supply the radial resolution the transfer consumes
($\nin\lesssim\Rc/2h$ on a grid of spacing $h$).

\paragraph*{Closed form.}
With $a=z_{m\ell}/\Rad$ and $b=z_{n\ell}/\Rc$, the Sturm--Liouville cross-product
identity evaluates the transfer matrices analytically, with no quadrature or
tabulation,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:transfer-closed}
\begin{aligned}
&\int_0^{R} r^2 j_\ell(ar)j_\ell(br)\,dr\\
&\qquad=\frac{R^2\bigl[a\,j_\ell'(aR)j_\ell(bR)-b\,j_\ell(aR)j_\ell'(bR)\bigr]}{b^2-a^2},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which at $R=\Rad$ simplifies further since $j_\ell(a\Rad)=j_\ell(z_{m\ell})=0$
(App.~\ref{app:scale}).

\subsection{Non-dimensionalization, covariance, completeness}
\label{sec:nondim}

The SIMPLE descriptors normalize the transferred coefficients by the local
amplitude scale at the adaptive radius,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dnlm}
d_{n\ell m}(\rr_0)=\frac{c'_{n\ell m}(\rr_0)}
{A_0(\Rad)\,\bar\rho_{\sigma,\mathrm{safe}}(\Rad)} ,
\end{equation}
which is dimensionless for every $\ell$ and, by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:dilation} and
\eqref{eq:transfer}, exactly invariant under uniform scaling in the continuum
(App.~\ref{app:nondim}). The ball average gives a smooth $\rho\to0$ limit,
$d_{n\ell m}=\mathcal O(\epsilon)$ and power spectrum $\sim\mathcal O(\epsilon^2)$
as $\rho\to\epsilon\rho$, with no overflow. The local scale $\bar\kF$ is retained
as an explicit feature --- the physically correct factorization, since exchange
obeys $E_x[\rho_\lambda]=\lambda E_x[\rho]$ while correlation requires the scale.

Because the transfer acts on the radial index $n$ and rotations act on the
magnetic index $m$, the two commute and $d_{n\ell m}$ transforms as a rank-$\ell$
spherical tensor (App.~\ref{app:nondim}). Rotational invariants follow by
contraction to total angular momentum zero --- the power spectrum
$\tilde P_{n\ell}=\sum_m d_{n\ell m}^2$, the bispectrum, and higher
Clebsch--Gordan trees --- which form a complete basis for rotationally invariant
multilinear functionals of the density. SIMPLE is thus a complete, orthogonal,
scale-invariant description of the density, systematically improvable as $\Rc$ and
the resolution increase.

\subsection{Gradient-energy expansion}
\label{sec:gea}

The gradient expansion of the exchange energy provides exact constraints on any
density functional~\cite{Antoniewicz_1985,Perdew_1996}. Its ingredients, the
reduced gradient $s=|\nabla\rho|/(2\kF\rho)$ and reduced Laplacian
$q=\nabla^2\rho/(4\kF^2\rho)$ (with $\kF=(3\pi^2\rho)^{1/3}$), are explicit
contractions of the SIMPLE features. Since $\nabla\rho$ is a vector (rank $1$) and
$\nabla^2\rho$ a scalar (rank $0$), they live in the $\ell=1$ and $\ell=0$ channels
of $d_{n\ell m}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sq}
s=c_s\sqrt{\sum_{m}\Big(\sum_n g^{(1)}_n\,d_{n1m}\Big)^2},\qquad
q=c_q\sum_n g^{(0)}_n\,d_{n00},
\end{equation}
with fixed parameter-free spectral weights $g^{(1)}_n,g^{(0)}_n$ and constants
$c_s,c_q$ (App.~\ref{app:gea}). The non-dimensionalization of $d$ already supplies
the $1/(2\kF\rho)$ and $1/(4\kF^2\rho)$ factors, so no empirical decoder is needed;
the reduced gradient is just the $\ell=1$ power of the spectrally-weighted features
and inherits their rotational and scale invariance. These reconstructed ingredients
feed the exchange functional's gradient expansion, whose coefficients are fixed by
the exact expansion rather than fit (given explicitly in Sec.~\ref{sec:hole}).
Extracting derivatives from a finite, truncated expansion is limited by the basis
resolution (Sec.~\ref{sec:results-gea}); within those bounds the SIMPLE-derived
ingredients agree with direct finite differences to $\sim1\%$.

\subsection{Operator projections}
\label{sec:operators}

The orthonormality of the basis converts integral functionals of the density into
finite contractions of the SIMPLE features $d_{n\ell m}$. For any
rotation-invariant $\nu$-point kernel $\mathcal K_\nu$, the functional
$\mathcal O_\nu[\rho]=\int\!\cdots\!\int\mathcal K_\nu\prod_i\rho(\rr_0+\rr_i)\,
d^3r_i$ becomes a multilinear form
$\sum T^{(\nu)}_{\{n_i\ell_i m_i\}}\prod_i d_{n_i\ell_i m_i}$ with constant
projected-kernel coefficients (non-dimensionalization only rescales $T^{(\nu)}$;
App.~\ref{app:operators}). The decisive example is the Coulomb operator $1/u$. Only
the monopole ($\ell=0$) average of the density,
$\bar\rho(r_0,u)=\sum_n C_n(r_0)R_n(u)$, survives its isotropic angular integral;
its coefficients are the $\ell=0$ SIMPLE features,
$C_n=A_0(\Rad)\,\bar\rho_{\sigma,\mathrm{safe}}(\Rad)\,d_{n00}$ [the dimensional
form of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dnlm}]. The operator itself is the radial Coulomb contraction
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:coulomb}
\begin{aligned}
\int_{B_{\Rc}}\!\frac{\rho(r_0+\uu)}{u}\,d^3u
&=4\pi\!\int_0^{\Rc}\!\bar\rho(r_0,u)\,u\,du\\
&=\sum_n w_n\,C_n(r_0),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with Coulomb-kernel weights $w_n=4\pi\int_0^{\Rc}R_n(u)\,u\,du$. The key point is
that the $1/u$ weight reduces the volume measure $u^2du$ to $u\,du$: the projection
keeps the long-range $1/r$ dependence that semilocal functionals miss --- it is
\emph{not} merely the average density $\bar\rho$, which discards it. The same
contraction with a model exchange hole in place of the bare kernel yields the
self-Coulomb $\Phi_S$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps-x}.

\subsection{Exchange-hole functional}
\label{sec:hole}

Exchange is exactly a hole integral,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exact-hole}
E_x[\rho]=\tfrac12\int d^3r_0\,\rho(r_0)\!\int d^3u\,\frac{n_x(r_0,\uu)}{u} ,
\end{equation}
where $\uu$ is the interelectronic displacement, $u=|\uu|$, and the exchange hole
$n_x(r_0,\uu)\le0$ obeys the normalization sum rule $\int d^3u\,n_x=-1$.
We model the hole as the actual neighboring density modulated by a single
universal shape, $n_x(r_0,\uu)=-\rho(r_0+\uu)\,S(\zeta(r_0)u)/\mathcal N(r_0)$,
with a single local inverse-length scale $\zeta(r_0)$ and normalization
$\mathcal N$. The $1/u$ weight then selects the monopole average, so the
self-energy density is a closed contraction of the monopole SIMPLE features
$\mathbf C=(C_0,C_1,\dots)$, $C_n=A_0(\Rad)\bar\rho_{\sigma,\mathrm{safe}}(\Rad)\,d_{n00}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:eps-x}
\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_x(r_0)&=-\tfrac12\,\frac{\Phi_S(\zeta)}{Q_S(\zeta)},\\
Q_S(\zeta)&=\bm\alpha(\zeta)\!\cdot\!\mathbf C,\qquad
\Phi_S(\zeta)=\bm\beta(\zeta)\!\cdot\!\mathbf C,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $Q_S$ is the number of electrons the model hole encloses, $\Phi_S$ its
self-Coulomb, and $\bm\alpha(\zeta),\bm\beta(\zeta)$ are precomputed envelope tables
with entries $\alpha_n(\zeta)=\int_0^\infty S(\zeta u)R_n(u)u^2du$ and
$\beta_n(\zeta)=\int_0^\infty S(\zeta u)R_n(u)u\,du$. Physically, exchange per
electron is $-\tfrac12$ the self-Coulomb of the enclosed density divided by the
number enclosed. The local scale $\zeta(r_0)$ is fixed by the on-top sum rule
$Q_S(\zeta)=2$ (the hole encloses one spin-paired pair). This construction is exact
in two limits by design:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] \textbf{HEG.} For uniform $\rho$ the monopole features $\mathbf C$ are
the homogeneous-gas coefficients; with the HEG hole shape
$S(x)=[3j_1(x)/x]^2=[j_0(x)+j_2(x)]^2$ the on-top equation
$Q_S(\zeta)=\bm\alpha(\zeta)\!\cdot\!\mathbf C=2$ is solved by $\zeta=\kF$, and the
envelope tables collapse $\varepsilon_x=-\tfrac12\Phi_S/Q_S=-\tfrac14\Phi_S(\kF)$ to
the Dirac value $\varepsilon_x^{\mathrm{unif}}=-\tfrac34(3/\pi)^{1/3}\rho^{1/3}$ ---
LDA exact by construction.
\item[(ii)] \textbf{Fermi--Amaldi.} Where fewer than two electrons are locally
available the sum rule cannot enclose a pair: $Q_S(\zeta)$ saturates below $2$ and
the modulating factor $\rho(r_0+\uu)$ is the full (spin) density, so
$\varepsilon_x=-\tfrac12\Phi_S/Q_S$ becomes the self-Coulomb of that density per
enclosed electron --- the self-interaction correction
$E_x=-\sum_\sigma J[\rho_\sigma]$ ($J$ the Hartree energy), exact for one electron
per spin (H, He).
\end{itemize}
The $\ell=1,0$ features deform the envelope $S$ through its small-$s$ slope, fixing
the gradient expansion of the resulting enhancement factor to fourth order,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fx}
F_x(s,q)=1+\tfrac{10}{81}\,s^2+\tfrac{146}{2025}\,q^2-\tfrac{73}{405}\,s^2 q
+\mathcal O(\nabla^6),
\end{equation}
the standard gradient-expansion coefficients~\cite{Antoniewicz_1985,Svendsen_1996}
imposed rather than fit. Collecting the limits, the exchange energy is the
GGA-style energy-density functional
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ex-gga}
\begin{aligned}
E_x[\rho]&=\int d^3r\,\rho\,\varepsilon_x^{\mathrm{unif}}(\rho)\,F_x(s,q),\\
F_x&=\frac{\varepsilon_x}{\varepsilon_x^{\mathrm{unif}}}
=-\frac{\Phi_S(\zeta)}{2\,Q_S(\zeta)\,\varepsilon_x^{\mathrm{unif}}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
whose enhancement factor gives $F_x\to1$ at uniform density [limit (i)], the
self-interaction correction in the few-electron limit [limit (ii)], and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fx} in between (Fig.~\ref{fig:fx}). Note that Eq.~\eqref{eq:fx} is the
pointwise gradient expansion; it excludes the re-summation over the density that the
hole construction performs (which bounds $F_x$ at large $s$), so as plotted it grows
without saturating.

\begin{figure}[tb]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fx_enhancement.pdf}
\caption{Fourth-order gradient-expansion (GEA) enhancement factor $F_x(s,q)$ that

symmetric it uses no higher multipoles, and carrying explicit $\ell\ge1$ hole
multipoles is the natural route to a richer functional (App.~\ref{app:hole}).

\paragraph*{Spin.}
Everything above is stated for the general spin-resolved hole. The exchange energy
decomposes exactly as
$E_x[\rho_\uparrow,\rho_\downarrow]=\tfrac12(E_x[2\rho_\uparrow]+E_x[2\rho_\downarrow])$,
so the spin-paired functional follows from the total-density expressions with the
on-top count of $2$, and the open-shell case is obtained by applying the
spin-scaling relation to each channel (App.~\ref{app:spin}).

\section{Numerical implementation}
\label{sec:impl}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph*{Convolutions and spectral operators.}
Every descriptor is a fixed-stencil convolution of the density. The gradient and
Laplacian ingredients of Sec.~\ref{sec:gea} are evaluated by parameter-free
spectral operators: the $\ell=1$ slope-at-origin sum
$\rho'(r_0)=\sum_n a^{(1)}_n R'_{n1}(0)$ (with a constant-annihilation correction
enforcing the uniform-density limit) and the $\ell=0$ Bessel-eigenvalue sum
$\nabla^2\rho(r_0)=-\sum_n a^{(0)}_n k_n^2 R_{n0}(0)$ ($k_n=z_{n0}/\Rc$,
$a^{(\ell)}_n$ the channel-$\ell$ coefficients). The Laplacian sum must be formed
\emph{per channel} --- the density is projected onto each $R_{n0}$ first, so the
coefficients $a^{(0)}_n$ decay for a smooth density, and the growing $k_n^2$ weights
are applied only afterward --- because folding those weights into a single kernel
\emph{before} the density integral builds a near-singular ($\sim\nabla^2\delta$)
operator that is catastrophically ill-conditioned (App.~\ref{app:gea}). So formed,
$\nabla^2\rho$ is recovered to $\sim1\%$ in the core/valence of smooth
(pseudopotential) atoms.

\paragraph*{Self-consistent potential.}
The exchange potential is the explicit variational derivative
$v_x=\delta E_x/\delta\rho$, which for convolutional descriptors is itself a
convolution over the kernel [Eq.~\eqref{eq:adjoint}], following
Ref.~\cite{Sahoo2024}; in the radial implementation it is the transpose of the
forward stencils. The on-top scale $\zeta(r_0)$ is obtained without an inner
self-consistency loop: $Q_S(\zeta)=\bm\alpha(\zeta)\cdot\mathbf C$ is monotonic in
$\zeta$, so inverting $Q_S=2$ pointwise on the precomputed envelope table is the
same fixed-stencil enclosed-charge solve used for the adaptive radius $\Rad$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:scale}), vectorized over all grid points (App.~\ref{app:hole-numerics}).
The asymptotic $-1/r$ exchange tail is recovered by an energy-neutral gauge fix plus a
low-density damping (App.~\ref{app:hole-numerics}). The $\ell=0$ Laplacian enters
the potential through the fourth-order GEA term [Eq.~\eqref{eq:fx}]; its adjoint
re-forms the singular $k_n^2$ kernel and is correspondingly stiff, mitigated in the
self-consistent cycle by a frozen-gradient dual loop --- the gradient/Laplacian
correction is held fixed on the stable bare-hole potential through the outer loop
(App.~\ref{app:hole-numerics}).

\paragraph*{Code and tests.}
The functional is implemented in the \texttt{atom-dft} finite-element radial
Kohn--Sham code~\cite{Kohn_1965,Hohenberg_1964} (all-electron and
pseudopotential). Descriptor tests use two independent grids --- a radial
finite-element grid and a 3D Cartesian finite-difference grid carrying
pseudo-H$_2$/N$_2$ densities. The test harness was driven in part by an agentic AI
assistant, with every reported result reproduced by the shipped scripts.

\subsection{SIMPLE feature tests}
\label{sec:results-features}

All feature tests run to $\ell=3$ at $R_c=3$~\AA, $\xi^*=2$, $\nout=8$. The number
of fixed-stencil kernels scales as $N_K=\nout(\ell_{\max}+1)^2$ --- linear in the
radial resolution $\nout$ and quadratic in $\ell_{\max}$ --- so the full descriptor
set here is $N_K=72$ convolutions of the density.

\emph{Orthogonality and the Nyquist limit.} The descriptors reproduce the analytic
HEG values $d^{\mathrm{HEG}}_{n00}=(-1)^n/(n+1)$, $d_{n\ell m}=0\,(\ell>0)$ to
$\sim2\times10^{-11}$ in the continuum and to $<4\times10^{-3}$ on the $h=0.2$~bohr
grid (SI Fig.~\ref{fig:heg}), degrading only as the channel count approaches the
Nyquist bound $\nin\lesssim\Rc/2h$.

\emph{Scale invariance.} Along $\rho\mapsto\lambda^3\rho(\lambda\rr)$ the relative
deviation at $\lambda=2$ is $0.35$--$1.7\%$ in the continuum, versus $170\%$ for the
untransformed fixed-window features (Fig.~\ref{fig:scale}); the residual is set by
the resolution bound and low-density window clamp of Sec.~\ref{sec:scale}.

\emph{Stability and covariance.} The measured low-density decay follows the
$\mathcal O(\epsilon)$/$\mathcal O(\epsilon^2)$ laws of Sec.~\ref{sec:nondim}
(fitted slope $2.000$; SI Fig.~\ref{fig:vacuum}). On 3D Cartesian grids the descriptors
converge as $\mathcal O(h^2)$ ($0.1$--$1.2\%$ at $h=0.2$~bohr), the orientation
spread of the invariants is $2$--$8\times10^{-4}$, and sub-grid registration stays
at the $10^{-3}$ level (App.~\ref{app:3d}) --- so $\sim0.2$~bohr spacings suffice.

\subsection{Gradient-expansion tests}
\label{sec:results-gea}

The SIMPLE-derived reduced gradient and Laplacian agree with direct finite
differences across the core and valence of real (pseudopotential) atoms: the
$s$ ratio is $0.996$--$1.000$ and the $q$ ratio is $\approx1.00$ for
He/Be/Mg/N/O. A fully self-consistent SIMPLE-PBE functional --- PBE evaluated on
the SIMPLE-reconstructed gradient --- reproduces standard PBE to within numerical
error (\PH{$\Delta E_x$ table, Phase D}).

\subsection{Exchange-hole functional}
\label{sec:results-hole}

Being spherically symmetric, the functional uses only the $N_C$ monopole channels
(plus the fixed gradient and Laplacian operators for the GEA limits), so a
self-consistent step costs $2N_C$ convolutions --- the forward descriptors
$\{C_n\}$ and the adjoint potential [Eq.~\eqref{eq:adjoint}] --- with
$N_C=\PH{24}$ here.

With the gauge fix of Sec.~\ref{sec:impl} the He HOMO eigenvalue is
$\PH{-0.910}$~Ha against the OEP $\PH{-0.913}$~Ha, giving the correct Koopmans
ionization potential. Figure~\ref{fig:vx} compares the self-consistent SIMPLE
exchange potential to the optimized effective
potential~\cite{Talman_1976,Krieger_1992} for \PH{He, Be, Ne}; the potentials agree
through core and valence and reproduce the $-1/r$ tail. Table~\ref{tab:energies}
reports exchange-only energies and Kohn--Sham gaps for all available single atoms
