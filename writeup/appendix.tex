\section{The orthonormal local expansion}
\label{app:basis}

We expand the density inside a ball $B_{\Rc}$ in a product basis of real spherical
harmonics and radial functions. The radial functions are spherical Bessel
functions truncated to the ball with Dirichlet (vanishing) boundary conditions,
\begin{equation}
R_{n\ell}(r)=\mathcal N_{n\ell}\,j_\ell\!\left(\frac{z_{n\ell}\,r}{\Rc}\right),
\qquad j_\ell\!\left(z_{n\ell}\right)=0,
\end{equation}
where $z_{n\ell}$ is the $(n{+}1)$th positive zero of $j_\ell$. The constants
$\mathcal N_{n\ell}$ are fixed by orthonormality with the radial measure $r^2dr$,
$\int_0^{\Rc} r^2 R_{n\ell}R_{n'\ell}\,dr=\delta_{nn'}$, which follows from the
Sturm--Liouville orthogonality of the Bessel functions. Why this basis? The
$j_\ell$ are eigenfunctions of the radial Laplacian,
$\nabla^2[j_\ell(kr)\Ylm]=-k^2 j_\ell(kr)\Ylm$, so derivatives act diagonally;
and the Dirichlet truncation makes the set complete
and orthonormal on the finite ball, which is what lets integrals collapse to sums
(App.~\ref{app:operators}).

The coefficients $c_{n\ell m}(\rr_0)=\int_{B_{\Rc}}K_{n\ell m}(\rr)
\rho_\sigma(\rr_0+\rr)\,d^3r$ are an exact, invertible representation of the local
density up to the truncation $n<\nin$, $\ell\le\ell_{\max}$: by orthonormality
$\rho_\sigma(\rr_0+\rr)=\sum_{n\ell m}c_{n\ell m}(\rr_0)K_{n\ell m}(\rr)$ inside
the ball. Each coefficient is a convolution of the density with a fixed kernel,
hence a single fixed stencil per channel on a grid.

A useful identity, used repeatedly, is the monopole integral of a basis function,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:An}
A_n(R)=\int_{B_R}K_{n00}\,d^3r=\sqrt{4\pi}\int_0^R r^2 R_{n0}(r)\,dr,
\end{equation}
which for the Dirichlet basis evaluates in closed form and gives the constant-density
projection $c_{n00}=\rho\,A_n(R)$, with the ratio $A_n/A_0=(-1)^n/(n+1)$ used below.

\section{The scale transform}
\label{app:scale}

This appendix derives the scale-invariance construct of Sec.~\ref{sec:scale}.

\paragraph*{The problem.}
We want features that do not change when the density is uniformly scaled,
$\rho_\lambda(\rr)=\lambda^3\rho(\lambda\rr)$ (this normalization keeps the
electron count fixed). Exact scale invariance is obtained by choosing the cutoff
to follow the local Fermi wavelength, $\Rc\propto\bar\kF^{-1}$; but a
density-dependent cutoff cannot be implemented as a single fixed convolution
stencil. SIMPLE keeps a fixed physical window and \emph{reconstructs} the
adaptive-window descriptors by a linear map.

\paragraph*{The dilation identity [Eq.~\eqref{eq:dilation}].}
Substitute $\rr\to\rr/\lambda$ in the projection Eq.~\eqref{eq:projection} and
evaluate the coefficients of the scaled density at the scaled cutoff $R/\lambda$.
Because each basis function depends on $r$ only through the dimensionless ratio
$z_{n\ell}r/R$, the integrand keeps its shape and only prefactors survive: the
$\lambda^3$ from $\rho_\lambda$, a $\lambda^{-3}$ from $d^3r$, and a
$\lambda^{-3/2}$ from the normalization $\mathcal N_{n\ell}\propto R^{-3/2}$
combine to the $\lambda^{3/2}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:dilation}. At $\ell=0$ this says
the fixed-window coefficients are samples of the spherical Bessel transform of the
unscaled density at \emph{dilated} wavevectors: scaling moves the sampling points
but loses no information, provided the window still contains the density.

\paragraph*{Why subtract the window mean [Eqs.~\eqref{eq:meansplit}--\eqref{eq:transfer}].}
The Dirichlet basis vanishes at $\Rc$, but a generic density does not, so directly
re-projecting carries slowly-decaying boundary oscillations (for the HEG, where
the density is pure boundary mismatch, this is a $10\%$ effect). Transferring the
fluctuation about the window average and adding back the mean analytically through
the monopole integrals $A_n$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:An}] removes the effect: for a uniform
density the fluctuation $\tilde c\equiv0$, so the transfer is exact at any
truncation --- this is why the HEG limit is exact by construction
(Fig.~\ref{fig:heg}).

\paragraph*{The closed form [Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer-closed}].}
The transfer matrix is the radial overlap of an adaptive-window basis function
with a fixed-window one. For two solutions $j_\ell(ar),j_\ell(br)$ of the
spherical Bessel equation ($a=z_{m\ell}/\Rad$, $b=z_{n\ell}/\Rc$) the
Sturm--Liouville cross-product identity integrates the overlap in closed form,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer-closed}, with no quadrature or tabulation. At $R=\Rad$ the
first bracket term vanishes because $j_\ell(a\Rad)=j_\ell(z_{m\ell})=0$.
(Tabulating in the ratio $\Rad/\Rc$ fails: the high-$n$ elements oscillate on the
scale $\pi/z_{n\ell}$, so naive interpolation is inaccurate --- the closed form is
load-bearing.) Near-degenerate pairs $a\approx b$ use the analytic limit of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer-closed}; when $\Rad=\Rc$ (clamped) the transfer is the
identity. Crucially $\mathsf M^{(\ell)}$ acts only on the radial index $n$,
identically for every $m$ --- the property that makes the transform commute with
rotations (App.~\ref{app:nondim}).

\section{Non-dimensionalization and invariance}
\label{app:nondim}

The descriptors are normalized at the adaptive radius,
$\varrho_{n\ell m}=c'_{n\ell m}/[A_0(\Rad)\bar\rho_{\sigma,\mathrm{safe}}(\Rad)]$. Under
scaling, $A_0(\Rad/\lambda)=\lambda^{-3/2}A_0(\Rad)$ and
$\bar\rho_\sigma\mapsto\lambda^3\bar\rho_\sigma$, so the denominator scales as
$\lambda^{3/2}$, exactly cancelling the $\lambda^{3/2}$ of the numerator from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:dilation}: $\varrho_{n\ell m}$ is invariant for every $\ell$, up to
$\rho_{\min}$ effects and the transfer truncation. (This corrects an earlier
convention that multiplied by $\bar\kF^\ell$, which actually multiplies the
fixed-$\xi$ features by $\lambda^\ell$ and breaks the invariance it was meant to
support; an order-unity balance across $\ell$ may instead be restored by a
\emph{constant} $(\xi^*)^{-\ell}$, which affects neither covariance nor
invariance.)

\paragraph*{Rotational covariance.}
Under a rotation $\mathcal R$, the raw coefficients transform as rank-$\ell$
tensors, $c_{n\ell m}\mapsto\sum_{m'}D^{(\ell)}_{mm'}(\mathcal R)c_{n\ell m'}$.
The transform preserves this because (i) $\Rad$ depends only on the invariant ball
average; (ii) $\mathsf M^{(\ell)}$ acts on $n$, not $m$; (iii) the mean split
touches only the invariant $(0,0)$ channel. Since the transfer acts on the radial
slot and the rotation on the magnetic slot, they commute,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:commute}
\bigl(\mathsf M^{(\ell)}\otimes\mathbb 1\bigr)\bigl(\mathbb 1\otimes D^{(\ell)}\bigr)
=\bigl(\mathbb 1\otimes D^{(\ell)}\bigr)\bigl(\mathsf M^{(\ell)}\otimes\mathbb 1\bigr),
\end{equation}
so $\varrho_{n\ell m}$ is a rank-$\ell$ tensor and any contraction to total angular
momentum zero is invariant (verified numerically to $\lesssim2\times10^{-15}$).

\paragraph*{Low-density limit.}
With the safe floor $\bar\rho_{\sigma,\mathrm{safe}}=(\bar\rho_\sigma^2+\rho_{\min}^2)^{1/2}$,
as $\rho\to\epsilon\rho$ the adaptive radius clamps at the window and the descriptors
reduce to fixed-window coefficients divided by a floor: $\varrho_{n\ell m}=\mathcal O(\epsilon)$
and $\tilde P_{n\ell}=\sum_m \varrho_{n\ell m}^2=\mathcal O(\epsilon^2)$, smoothly and
without overflow, with crossover at $\epsilon^*=\rho_{\min}/\bar\rho_\sigma$.

\section{Operator projections}
\label{app:operators}

\paragraph*{Multilinear functionals as sums.}
Any $\nu$-point integral functional with a rotation-invariant kernel,
$\mathcal O_\nu[\rho](\rr_0)=\int_{B_{\Rc}}\!\cdots\!\int\mathcal K_\nu(\rr_1,\dots,\rr_\nu)
\prod_i\rho(\rr_0+\rr_i)\,d^3r_i$, becomes, on inserting the expansion
$\rho(\rr_0+\rr)=\sum c_{n\ell m}K_{n\ell m}(\rr)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal O_\nu[\rho]&=\sum_{\{n_i\ell_i m_i\}}T^{(\nu)}_{\{n_i\ell_i m_i\}}\prod_{i=1}^\nu c_{n_i\ell_i m_i},\\
T^{(\nu)}&=\int\!\cdots\!\int\mathcal K_\nu\prod_i K_{n_i\ell_i m_i}\,d^3r_i .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The projected-kernel tensor $T^{(\nu)}$ is computed once. Rotation invariance of
$\mathcal K_\nu$ forces the magnetic indices to couple to total angular momentum
zero, which is exactly Clebsch--Gordan coupling; the power spectrum ($\nu=2$),
bispectrum ($\nu=3$), and higher CG trees therefore form a complete basis for
rotationally invariant multilinear functionals. Non-dimensionalization only
rescales $T^{(\nu)}$ by scalar prefactors and does not change the admissible class.

\paragraph*{The Coulomb operator is a monopole.}
For the exchange-relevant kernel $\mathcal K=1/u$ acting on the density at
displacement $\uu$, the angular integral $\oint d\Omega_u\,(1/u)\,
\rho(\rr_0+\uu)$ projects out only the $\ell=0$ component, because $1/u$ is
isotropic and the higher harmonics integrate to zero. Hence the projected Coulomb
operator acts through the monopole descriptors alone,
\begin{equation}
\bar\rho(r_0,u)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint\rho(r_0+\uu)\,d\Omega_u=\sum_n C_n(r_0)R_n(u) ,
\end{equation}
with monopole descriptors $C_n=(P_n\rho)(r_0)$, where $\{P_n\}$ are fixed window
operators. The Coulomb operator acting on this monopole density is then a weighted
sum over the descriptors [Eq.~\eqref{eq:coulomb}],
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\int_{B_{\Rc}}\!\frac{\rho(r_0+\uu)}{u}\,d^3u
&=4\pi\!\int_0^{\Rc}\!\bar\rho(r_0,u)\,u\,du\\
&=\sum_n w_n C_n(r_0),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $w_n=4\pi\int_0^{\Rc}R_n(u)\,u\,du$. The $1/u$ weight reduces the radial
measure from $u^2du$ to $u\,du$: this is how the long-range $1/r$ structure that
semilocal functionals miss enters, and it is the foundation of the exchange-hole
functional below.

\section{Reduced gradient from the expansion}
\label{app:gea}

The gradient expansion of exchange,
$E_x=\int\varepsilon_x^{\mathrm{LDA}}(\rho)\bigl[1+\mu s^2+\dots\bigr]\rho\,d^3r$,
requires the reduced gradient $s=|\nabla\rho|/(2\kF\rho)$, with
$\kF=(3\pi^2\rho)^{1/3}$. We extract it from the SIMPLE expansion with a
parameter-free spectral operator.

\paragraph*{Gradient (slope at origin).}
The density at the expansion center along a ray is built from the $\ell=1$
channel; its slope at $r_0$ is $\rho'(r_0)=\sum_n a^{(1)}_n R'_{n1}(0)$, where
$a^{(1)}_n$ are the channel-$1$ radial expansion coefficients and
$R'_{n1}(0)=\mathcal N_{n1}k_n/3$ (the small-argument behavior $j_1(x)\approx x/3$),
$k_n=z_{n1}/\Rc$. This is a fixed linear operator on the gridded density, and ---
growing only as $k_n^1$ --- it is well-conditioned, so the slope is recovered
directly without any per-channel reformation.

\paragraph*{Why not the Laplacian.}
The same construction would give the reduced Laplacian from the $\ell=0$ channel via
$\nabla^2[R_{n0}]=-k_n^2 R_{n0}$, i.e.\ $\nabla^2\rho(r_0)=-\sum_n a^{(0)}_n k_n^2
R_{n0}(0)$. The forward sum can be stabilized by forming it \emph{per channel} (the
$a^{(0)}_n$ decay for a smooth density, the growing $k_n^2$ weights applied
afterward), but its variational adjoint --- needed for the self-consistent
potential --- re-forms the single $k_n^2$ kernel $\sum_n(-k_n^2 R_{n0}(0))R_{n0}(u)$,
a near-singular ($\sim\nabla^2\delta$) object whose huge oscillatory values are
catastrophically ill-conditioned. The $k_n^2$ growth makes this fatal for $\ell=0$,
and no practical regularization kept the self-consistent cycle stable across cutoffs.
We therefore stop the expansion at second order and do not carry a Laplacian channel;
the functional uses only the $\ell=1$ gradient.

\paragraph*{Constant annihilation and the uniform limit.}
The $\ell=1$ gradient operator ends with
$\mathsf O\to\mathsf O-\mathrm{diag}(\sum_j\mathsf O_{ij})$, forcing
$\mathsf O[\text{const}]=0$ (the gradient of a uniform density vanishes; the
subtraction also removes the DC truncation residual), so the reduced gradient
vanishes for the HEG by construction.

\paragraph*{In SIMPLE features.}
Dividing this dimensional operator by the non-dimensionalization amplitude turns it
into the feature contraction of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sq}: the spectral weights are
$g^{(1)}_n\propto R'_{n1}(0)$ (slope-at-origin) acting on the $\ell=1$ features
$\varrho_{n1m}$. The reduced gradient is the norm of the $\ell=1$ contraction --- hence the
$\ell=1$ power of the spectrally-weighted features.

\paragraph*{The $10/81$ coefficient.}
For the ball-normalized reduced gradient the second-order gradient-expansion
coefficient comes out to the standard $\mu=10/81$~\cite{Antoniewicz_1985} on the
single-scale path that atoms occupy, with $\kappa=s_{\mathrm{SIMPLE}}/s_{\mathrm{phys}}\to1$
in the high-density limit --- derived, not fit. Extracting derivatives from a
finite truncated expansion has intrinsic limits: the spectral operators require a
smooth density (a bare nuclear cusp breaks them, but pseudopotential densities are
smooth) and the full window with $\sim40$ channels; the far tail degrades where
the finite-difference reference is itself noisy.

\section{The exchange-hole functional: full derivation}
\label{app:hole}

We reconstruct the functional from the exact hole, flagging each degree of freedom
(DOF) that is dropped or constrained, so the model's location relative to the exact
functional is explicit.

\paragraph*{Exact starting point.}
The exchange energy is exactly Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-hole} with
$n_x(r_0,\uu)=-|\rho_1(r_0,r_0+\uu)|^2/\rho(r_0)$, $\rho_1$ the one-particle
density matrix. The hole obeys the sum rule $\int d^3u\,n_x=-1$, the on-top value
$n_x(r_0,\mathbf 0)=-\tfrac12\rho(r_0)$, and $n_x\le0$. The full object is a
two-point function with arbitrary angular dependence in $\uu$.

\paragraph*{DOF \#1 --- convolutional ansatz.}
We factor the hole into the actual neighboring density, a single universal shape
$S$, and two local scalars:
$n_x(r_0,\uu)=-\rho(r_0+\uu)S(\zeta(r_0)u)/\mathcal N(r_0)$. The neighboring-density
factor retains genuine non-local information; the \emph{shape} is collapsed to one
function plus a local inverse-length scale $\zeta$ and a normalization $\mathcal N$.

\paragraph*{DOF \#2 --- monopole projection (decisive).}
Inserting the ansatz into Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-hole}, the $1/u$ weight selects the
spherical average of $\rho(r_0+\uu)$, so only the $\ell=0$ multipole survives
(App.~\ref{app:operators}): $\bar\rho(r_0,u)=\sum_n C_n(r_0)R_n(u)$. The model is
intrinsically a spherically symmetric hole; \emph{every $\ell\ge1$ hole multipole
is dropped here.} This is the largest DOF loss and is the source of the residual
open-shell error.

\paragraph*{Closed kernel (no DOF lost).}
With $S$ fixed, the $u$-integrals precompute into the envelope tables
$\alpha_n(\zeta)=\int_0^\infty S(\zeta u)R_n(u)u^2du$ and
$\beta_n(\zeta)=\int_0^\infty S(\zeta u)R_n(u)u\,du$. The number enclosed and the
self-Coulomb of the enclosed density are linear contractions
$Q_S(\zeta)=\bm\alpha(\zeta)\!\cdot\!\mathbf C$ and
$\Phi_S(\zeta)=\bm\beta(\zeta)\!\cdot\!\mathbf C$,
giving the boxed self-energy $\varepsilon_x=-\tfrac12\Phi_S/Q_S$
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:eps-x}]: exchange per electron is $-\tfrac12$ the self-Coulomb of
the enclosed density divided by the number enclosed.

\paragraph*{DOF \#3 --- closing the scale, and the exact limits.}
One scalar per point remains, fixed by the on-top sum rule $Q_S(\zeta)=2$ (a
spin-paired pair). This is where the limits enter:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{HEG/LDA.} Uniform $\rho\Rightarrow\bar\rho$ constant; with the HEG hole
$S(x)=[3j_1(x)/x]^2=[j_0(x)+j_2(x)]^2$, $Q_S=2\Rightarrow \zeta=\kF$ and
$\varepsilon_x\to\varepsilon_x^{\mathrm{LDA}}$. Exact by construction; here $S$ is
frozen to the HEG shape.
\item \emph{Fermi--Amaldi.} Low density / one electron $\Rightarrow Q_S<2$,
giving the self-interaction-corrected form with the full enclosed density --- exact
for H/He.
\end{itemize}
The ``2'' is a spin assumption (a paired pair); the open-shell case is handled by
the spin decomposition of App.~\ref{app:spin}.

\paragraph*{Recovering the gradient expansion.}
Inhomogeneity is returned to the shape by adding a single Bessel mode that
vanishes at the origin (preserving on-top and HEG),
$S(x;\chi)=[\,j_0(x)+j_2(x)+\chi\,\varphi(x)]^2$ with $\varphi=j_1$ and a single
deformation amplitude $\chi$ linear in the SIMPLE reduced gradient, $\chi\propto
s^2$ [Eq.~\eqref{eq:sq}]. The amplitude vanishes for a uniform density (preserving
LDA), and its small-$s$ slope is fixed by the second-order gradient expansion
($\mu=10/81$). The parameter-free functional uses only the monopole and $\ell=1$
gradient features and so is spherically symmetric.

\paragraph*{Calibrating the deformation (one-time HEG response).}
The map from the reduced gradient to the envelope amplitude is fixed, not fit. To
linear order the envelope deformation shifts the enhancement factor by
$\delta F_x=R\,\chi$, where the \emph{envelope response} $R\equiv\partial
F_x/\partial\chi$ is evaluated once at the homogeneous gas: perturb the amplitude
$\chi$ about the bare hole ($\chi=0$), re-solve the on-top scale $Q_S(\zeta)=2$, and
read off the change in $F_x=\varepsilon_x/\varepsilon_x^{\mathrm{unif}}$. The
amplitude is then chosen so $R\,\chi$ equals the second-order gradient-expansion term
$\tfrac{10}{81}s^2$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fx}, i.e.\ $\chi=R^{-1}\tfrac{10}{81}s^2$
[Eq.~\eqref{eq:amp}]. The key point is that $F_x$ is evaluated on the \emph{adaptive}
radius $\Rad=\xi^*/\kF$ reached by the scale transform of Sec.~\ref{sec:scale}, so it
depends only on the dimensionless profile and $R$ is a single density- and
cutoff-independent constant (computed once); under a fixed window the same response
drifts with density and cutoff and would require a one-parameter regression over
reference atoms. The deformed functional therefore reproduces Eq.~\eqref{eq:fx} by
construction while the bare-hole limits survive. The large-$s$ divergence of the
expansion is regularized by saturating the amplitude,
$\chi\to\chi_{\max}\tanh(\chi/\chi_{\max})$, with $\chi_{\max}$ fixed by the
Lieb--Oxford bound $F_x\le1.804$; acting on the amplitude it leaves the second-order
slope and the on-top value untouched. Because the $\ell=1$ gradient adjoint grows only
as $k_n^1$ and is well-conditioned, the resulting potential is self-consistent and
stable without any auxiliary loop --- the central reason the expansion is stopped at
second order rather than carrying the stiff $\ell=0$ Laplacian channel
(App.~\ref{app:gea}).

\paragraph*{Plane-wave (HEG) verification of the second-order limit.}
Perturbing the uniform gas with a density wave $\rho_0(1+\alpha\cos kz)$ (analytic
monopole, operator-free) shows the bare hole is \emph{monopole-blind} to the
gradient: its $s^2$ response vanishes identically at the pure-gradient phase. The
$\ell=1$ deformation therefore supplies the \emph{entire} second-order term, and the
exact slope $\mu=10/81$ is recovered as $\rho\to$~HEG --- an independent check that
the construction carries the gradient expansion at second order.

\section{Spin conventions}
\label{app:spin}

Exchange acts within each spin channel, so the spin-resolved energy follows from
the spin-scaling relation
$E_x[\rho_\uparrow,\rho_\downarrow]=\tfrac12\bigl(E_x[2\rho_\uparrow]+E_x[2\rho_\downarrow]\bigr)$.
For a spin-paired system $\rho_\uparrow=\rho_\downarrow=\rho/2$, this reduces to the
total-density expressions used in the main text, with the on-top count of $2$. For
open shells we evaluate the spin-restricted functional on $2\rho_\sigma$ in each
channel and combine; the Hartree--Fock/OEP reference used for comparison is itself
spin-restricted, which under-counts open shells, so the unrestricted-exact
correction is applied when comparing.

\section{Self-consistent potential and numerical details}
\label{app:hole-numerics}

\paragraph*{Convolutional adjoint potential.}
Each monopole descriptor is a convolution of the density with a fixed kernel,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:descriptor-conv}
C_n(\rr)=\int K_n(\rr'-\rr)\,\rho(\rr')\,d^3r'=(K_n*\rho)(\rr),
\end{equation}
so that $\delta C_n(\rr')/\delta\rho(\rr)=K_n(\rr-\rr')$. With the exchange energy
$E_x=\int\rho\,\varepsilon_x[\{C_n\}]\,d^3r$, the potential is the explicit
variational derivative, following Ref.~\cite{Sahoo2024},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adjoint}
\begin{aligned}
v_x(\rr)&=\frac{\delta E_x}{\delta\rho(\rr)}\\
&=\varepsilon_x(\rr)+\sum_n\int d^3r'\,\rho(\rr')\,
\frac{\partial\varepsilon_x(\rr')}{\partial C_n}\,K_n(\rr-\rr') .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The second term is itself a convolution over the kernel: each descriptor's
contribution is $K_n$ applied to $\rho\,\partial\varepsilon_x/\partial C_n$. In the
radial finite-element implementation this convolution is the transpose
$P_n^{\mathsf T}$ of the forward stencil $P_n$, weighted by the quadrature measure
$w=4\pi r^2 w_{\mathrm{quad}}$ ($w_{\mathrm{quad}}$ the radial quadrature weights),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adjoint-discrete}
v_x=\varepsilon_x+\sum_n \frac{P_n^{\mathsf T}\!\bigl[w\,\rho\,
\partial\varepsilon_x/\partial C_n\bigr]}{w} ,
\end{equation}
exact against finite differences of the energy.

\paragraph*{On-top scale by enclosed-charge inversion.}
The on-top scale is \emph{not} found by a nested self-consistent iteration. The
$S$-weighted enclosed charge
$Q_S(\zeta)=\bm\alpha(\zeta)\cdot\mathbf C=\int_0^\infty S(\zeta u)\,\bar\rho(r_0,u)\,4\pi u^2\,du$
uses the precomputed envelope table $\alpha_n(\zeta)$, so for the fixed monopole
descriptors $\mathbf C$ it is a single matrix--vector product on a $\zeta$-grid. As
$\zeta\to0$, $S\to1$ and $Q_S$ approaches the full window charge; as
$\zeta\to\infty$, $Q_S\to0$. Because $Q_S(\zeta)$ is monotonic, $Q_S(\zeta)=2$ has a
unique root recovered by one-dimensional inversion of the precomputed curve --- the
identical fixed-stencil enclosed-charge machinery that solves $g(\Rad)=\xi^*$ for
the adaptive radius (App.~\ref{app:scale}). The solve is therefore pointwise,
non-iterative, and vectorized over all grid points, adding no loop to the
Kohn--Sham cycle. (Confirmed in the implementation: the self-consistent functional
reproduces the direct-integral hole on the converged density.)

\paragraph*{Adjoint conditioning and the second-order cutoff.}
The exchange-kernel response $\delta v_x/\delta\rho$ is dominated by the gradient
deformation. The $\ell=1$ adjoint grows only as $k_n^1$ and is well-conditioned, so
the second-order functional converges self-consistently across all atoms tested with
no auxiliary loop. A fourth-order Laplacian term would instead enter through the
$\ell=0$ spectral Laplacian, whose adjoint re-forms the singular $k_n^2$ kernel and is
catastrophically ill-conditioned (App.~\ref{app:gea}); attempts to stabilize it
(per-channel forward, frozen-correction outer loops) did not hold across cutoffs.
This is why the expansion is stopped at second order.

\paragraph*{The $-1/r$ tail.}
The exact exchange potential decays as $-1/r$. The model potential is made to
recover this by an energy-neutral gauge fix (subtracting the asymptotic constant)
together with a low-density damping $f=\rho^2/(\rho^2+\rho_c^2)$ ($\rho_c$ a small
damping density), which restores the $-1/r$ tail and the correct Koopmans
ionization potential without changing the energy.

\section{Three-dimensional Cartesian-grid validation}
\label{app:3d}

The entire pipeline is run on genuine 3D Cartesian lattices, with window
coefficients by direct kernel sums, ball averages by partial-cell shell sums
(linear-ramp weights normalized by their own sum, keeping the HEG limit exact),
and the adaptive radius, transfer, and non-dimensionalization as in 1D. Test
densities are pseudo-diatomics: pseudo-H$_2$ (two analytic $1s$ atoms,
$\rho=e^{-2r}/\pi$, $b=1.40$~bohr; nuclear cusps) and pseudo-N$_2$ (two nitrogen
pseudopotential valence densities from the radial DFT solver, GGA-PBE,
$b=2.074$~bohr; smooth). A quasi-exact continuum reference is built per-atom in an
aligned frame and rotated with real Wigner-$D$ matrices.

The descriptors converge as $\mathcal O(h^2)$ ($0.1$--$1.2\%$ at $h=0.2$~bohr,
$0.03$--$0.3\%$ at $h=0.1$); under 24 random rotations the per-channel deviation
from the covariant prediction stays at the static-quadrature level
($\lesssim8\times10^{-3}$) and the rotation-invariant power-spectrum spread is only
$2$--$8\times10^{-4}$; symmetry-forbidden odd-$\ell$ channels at the bond midpoint
vanish to machine precision ($\lesssim2\times10^{-16}$); and a sub-grid registration
sweep stays at $\sim\!1.0\times10^{-3}$. The lattice scale-invariance test tracks the 1D radial behavior
with the same two breakdown mechanisms. Thus $\sim0.2$~bohr grid spacings, typical
of real-space pseudopotential codes, are sufficient.

\section{Practical limits of the SIMPLE features on real-space grids}
\label{app:stress}

The validation above establishes that the descriptors are convergent and covariant;
this appendix maps the \emph{practical} limits --- where each feature family, used as
a functional ingredient, stays invariant on a realistic grid and where it fails. We
stress four ingredients through $\ell=3$ --- the reduced gradient $s$, the reduced
Laplacian $q$, the power spectrum $\tilde P_{n\ell}$ (32 entries), and the bispectrum
(all triangle triples, 6656 entries) --- on five superposition densities built from
single-atom radial densities: pseudo-H$_2$, pseudo-N$_2$, pseudo-H$_2$O (O$+$2H at
$0.96$~\AA, $104.5^\circ$), and FCC bulk Al and Pt (clusters filling the window). The
cutoff is fixed at $\Rc=6$~bohr; the realistic spacings are $h=0.1,0.2,0.3$~bohr; the
ground truth is the analytic continuum-quadrature reference (for $\tilde P$ and the
bispectrum) and a finite-difference gradient/Laplacian of the analytic density (for
$s,q$). We flag any relative error $>10\%$ whose feature magnitude is above a
per-group floor (a large relative error on a near-zero feature carries no
information). The four tests are translation (sub-grid registration), rotation
(random rigid rotations), uniform scaling, and grid-spacing sensitivity; the script
\texttt{invariant\_stress\_test.py} reproduces every number and figure.

\paragraph*{Rotation and translation are robust.}
Across 8 random rotations the rotation-invariant features ($s,q,\tilde P$, bispectrum)
hold to $\le1.4\%$ at $h=0.2$ for every system and center (median $<0.5\%$) --- the
grid breaks rotational symmetry only at the static-quadrature level. Under sub-grid
registration shifts the deviation stays $\le1\%$ except at the bare pseudo-H$_2$
nuclear cusp, where it reaches $6.5\%$ (the cusp is the hardest case for any uniform
grid; pseudopotential and bulk densities are smooth and stay $\lesssim1\%$).

\paragraph*{Grid-spacing: $\tilde P$ and the bispectrum converge; $q$ is a
reconstruction problem, not a grid problem.}
The power spectrum and bispectrum converge as $\mathcal O(h^2)$ toward the continuum
(Fig.~\ref{fig:stress-grid}), reaching the $10\%$ flag only for the cusped pseudo-H$_2$
at the coarsest $h=0.3$; at $h=0.2$ all systems are within $\sim$6\% and the smooth
ones within $\sim$2\%. The \emph{grid-discretization} error of $s$ and $q$ is likewise
small. Their limitation is instead the \emph{reconstruction} fidelity (continuum
feature versus the exact derivative): $s$ is faithful in molecules ($\le4\%$ for
N$_2$, H$_2$O), but $q$ --- the $\ell=0$ Bessel-eigenvalue contraction --- is
unreliable in dense environments, off by tens to hundreds of times at the bulk metal
sites ($\sim\!60\times$ for Al, $\sim\!900\times$ for the Pt octahedral interstitial).
This is the practical confirmation that the dimensionless Laplacian cannot be carried
as a functional ingredient (Sec.~\ref{sec:gea}, App.~\ref{app:gea}); the high-symmetry
bulk sites are inversion centers where $\nabla\rho\equiv0$, so $s$ itself vanishes
there and its relative error is not informative, whereas $q$ is genuinely large and
genuinely wrong.

\paragraph*{Uniform scaling: an inner-channel resolution limit, not a clamp.}
At the grid-matched channel count $\nin=16$ (the $\nin\!\simeq\!\Rc/2h$ pairing for
$h=0.2$, $\Rc=6$) the dimensionless features deviate under uniform scaling more than
the raw descriptors of Fig.~\ref{fig:scale} would suggest --- the apparent
deviations grow with $|\lambda-1|$ and are largest for the bispectrum and the dense
bulk systems. This is \emph{not} the adaptive-radius clamp: across every system and
scaling here $\Rad$ stays well below $\Rc$ (e.g.\ $0.3$--$3$~bohr; never clamped). Nor
is it a grid artifact --- the continuum-quadrature descriptors show the same
deviation. It is the \emph{inner-channel resolution} bound of the scale transform,
$k^*\lambda\lesssim\nin\pi/\Rc$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:scale}): the transfer that
reconstructs the dilated-window descriptors needs enough inner channels to represent
the dilated sampling, and $\nin=16$ at $\Rc=6$ exhausts that resolution sooner than
the $\nin\!\ge\!32$ used for the continuum results of Fig.~\ref{fig:scale}. Raising
$\nin$ collapses the deviation back to the Fig.~\ref{fig:scale} level
(Fig.~\ref{fig:stress-scaleres}): the descriptor-vector deviation at $\lambda=1.67$
falls from $\sim\!19\%$ ($\nin=16$) to $\sim\!1\%$ ($\nin=32$) to $\sim\!0.2\%$
($\nin=48$), exactly the documented convergence. (The per-entry \emph{maximum}
relative error of Fig.~\ref{fig:stress-scale} further amplifies this over the
vector-norm metric, since it is dominated by the smallest, near-floor power-spectrum
and bispectrum entries.) The practical statement is therefore not that the features
fail to be scale-invariant, but that \emph{scale-invariant use of the
transfer-reconstructed features requires $\nin\gtrsim32$} --- i.e.\ a grid fine enough
($h\lesssim0.1$~bohr at $\Rc=6$) to supply the inner channels, or a channel count
decoupled from the grid spacing.

\paragraph*{Summary.}
At $h\le0.2$~bohr the power spectrum, the bispectrum, and the reduced gradient $s$ are
practically usable under rotation, translation, and grid refinement; the reduced
Laplacian $q$ is not reliably reconstructable (catastrophically so in bulk metals,
where its $\ell=0$ eigenvalue sum is ill-conditioned), consistent with the $q$-free,
second-order functional of the main text. Scale invariance holds as the theory
predicts, but only once the inner-channel count resolves the dilation
($\nin\gtrsim32$); at the coarse grid-matched $\nin=16$ it is the apparent soft spot,
so adequate radial resolution (or an $\nin$ decoupled from $h$) is the requirement for
applying the transferred features to strongly scaled or condensed-phase densities.

\section{Parameter selection: window, resolution, scale range, and cost}
\label{app:params}

The limits of App.~\ref{app:stress} fix the few free parameters of the descriptor ---
the window $\Rc$, the grid spacing $h$, the inner- and exposed-channel counts
$\nin,\nout$, and the angular cutoff $\ell_{\max}$ --- through two requirements: a
target energy accuracy (which sets $\Rc$ and $\nout$) and a target scale-invariance
range (which couples $h$, $\nin$, $\nout$, and the cost). We make both quantitative and
adopt a single production setting, $\Rc=6$~bohr and $\nout=10$, used in the rest of the
paper.

\paragraph*{Scale range, resolution, and cost are one relation.}
On a grid of spacing $h$ the inner channels resolvable below the Nyquist wavevector
$\pi/h$ number $\nin=\Rc/h$ (the highest channel $k_{\nin}=\nin\pi/\Rc=\pi/h$ sits
exactly at Nyquist). The scale transform reconstructs the dilated-window descriptors
only while the dilated sampling stays within that resolution, $k^*\lambda\lesssim
\nin\pi/\Rc$ with $k^*\simeq\nout\pi/\Rc$ the highest \emph{exposed} wavevector, i.e.\
the features stay scale-invariant up to a dilation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lammax}
\Lambda_{\max}\;\simeq\;\frac{\nin}{\nout}\;=\;\frac{\Rc}{h\,\nout}.
\end{equation}
This is confirmed directly (Fig.~\ref{fig:design}a): at fixed $\nout=10$, the
descriptor-vector deviation $D(\lambda)$ stays $\lesssim1\%$ until $\lambda$ approaches
$\Lambda_{\max}=\nin/\nout$ and rises beyond it, with the usable range growing linearly
in $\nin$ ($\Lambda_{\max}\approx2.0,3.0,6.0$ at $h=0.3,0.2,0.1$~bohr). The cost is set
by the same $\nin$: each descriptor is one fixed-stencil convolution per radial channel
and angular index, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nconv}
N_{\mathrm{conv}}=\nin(\ell_{\max}+1)^2=\frac{\Rc}{h}(\ell_{\max}+1)^2 ,
\end{equation}
the transfer to $\nout$ being a cheap per-point matrix product. Eliminating $h$ between
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:lammax}--\eqref{eq:nconv} gives the design rule directly: to be
scale-invariant over a dilation $\Lambda$ with $\nout$ exposed channels through
$\ell_{\max}$ requires
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:designrule}
h\le\frac{\Rc}{\nout\,\Lambda},\qquad
N_{\mathrm{conv}}\ge\nout\,\Lambda\,(\ell_{\max}+1)^2 .
\end{equation}
For $\Rc=6$, $\nout=10$, $\ell_{\max}=3$ (so $(\ell_{\max}+1)^2=16$) this is
$N_{\mathrm{conv}}=320,480,960$ convolutions at $h=0.3,0.2,0.1$~bohr (set by $\nin$, not
$\nout$), buying $\Lambda_{\max}\approx2.0,3.0,6.0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:design}b) --- the
coarsest practical grid ($h=0.3$) already covers a $2.0\times$ linear (${\sim}8\times$
density) dilation at $320$ convolutions, and halving $h$ buys proportionally more range
at proportionally more cost.

\paragraph*{The cost is set by the scale range, not the grid.}
A subtlety worth making explicit: $\nin$ is a \emph{design choice} --- the number of
inner channels one elects to compute --- bounded above only by the grid through
$\nin\le\Rc/h$. The sequence $N_{\mathrm{conv}}=320,480,960$ above corresponds to
spending the \emph{full} grid resolution $\nin=\Rc/h$ at each $h$, which is why it grows
as the grid refines (and buys the larger $\Lambda_{\max}=2,3,6$). But for a \emph{fixed}
target scale range one simply fixes $\nin=\nout\Lambda$; the convolution count
$N_{\mathrm{conv}}=\nout\Lambda(\ell_{\max}+1)^2$ is then independent of $h$, and the
grid need only be fine enough to resolve it, $h\le\Rc/\nin$. Numerically, holding
$\nin=2\nout=20$ fixed (so $N_{\mathrm{conv}}=320$ at $\ell_{\max}=3$, for the full
$\Lambda_{\max}=2$): the scale deviation $D(\lambda)$ is essentially
\emph{$h$-independent} ($D(\lambda{=}2)\approx0.035$ at $h=0.3,0.2,0.1,0.05$~bohr), while
refining $h$ only sharpens those same channels (their deviation from the continuum falls
$0.5\%\!\to\!0.01\%$ from $h=0.3$ to $0.05$). So a finer grid does \emph{not} require
more convolutions for a given scale range --- it improves accuracy at fixed cost.
Conversely, extending $\Lambda$ means raising $\nin$ (more convolutions) and refining $h$
to resolve it; reducing the angular cutoff is the other lever, $N_{\mathrm{conv}}=
\nin(\ell_{\max}+1)^2 = 320,180,80$ at $\nin=20$ for $\ell_{\max}=3,2,1$. The full
$\ell\le3$ feature set at $\Lambda=2$ is thus a fixed $320$ convolutions on any grid
$h\le0.3$~bohr.

\paragraph*{An energy scale fixes $\Rc$ and $\nout$.}
To anchor the two remaining counts to a physical tolerance we evaluate the Hartree
self-energy of the hydrogen $1s$ density ($\rho=e^{-2r}/\pi$, exact $J=5/16$~Ha) through
the SIMPLE windowed Coulomb projection [Eq.~\eqref{eq:coulomb}], $V_H(r_0)=\sum_n w_n
C_n$ with the monopole window coefficients $C_n$ in an $\nout$-channel basis and Coulomb
weights $w_n=4\pi\int_0^{\Rc}R_n(u)\,u\,du$. Two truncations control the error
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hartree}): the window $\Rc$ (the Coulomb tail beyond it is dropped) and
$\nout$ (the radial-basis resolution of the enclosed density). The window floor reaches
the milli-Hartree scale only at $\Rc\gtrsim6$~bohr --- $-1.7$~mHa at $\Rc=5$,
$\mathbf{-0.37}$~\textbf{mHa at} $\boldsymbol{\Rc=6}$, $-0.01$~mHa at $\Rc=8$ --- so
$\Rc=6$ is the smallest window consistent with sub-milli-Hartree Coulomb energetics, and
no $\nout$ can rescue a shorter window ($\Rc=4$ plateaus at $-7.9$~mHa). At $\Rc=6$ the
radial-basis error falls below $1$~mHa already by $\nout\approx8$ and effectively
reaches the window floor by $\nout\approx10$ ($-0.49$~mHa at $\nout=10$; further channels
gain little, the residual being the tail). Hence $\Rc=6$~bohr and $\nout=10$ deliver the
Coulomb energy to ${\sim}0.5$~mHa with margin --- the production setting.

\paragraph*{The energy floor on a practical grid.}
The $-0.5$~mHa figure above is the continuum value of the projection; on a real
Cartesian grid it acquires a discretization error that depends on the spacing $h$ and on
where the nucleus sits relative to the lattice (sub-grid registration). Evaluating the
same H Hartree energy on a 3D grid (the windowed-Coulomb kernel convolved with the
gridded density) for four registrations --- nucleus on a lattice point, on a face
center, on the body center, and a generic offset --- shows the error fanning out from
the continuum floor as $h$ coarsens (Fig.~\ref{fig:hartreegrid}): all registrations
agree to $0.02$~mHa at $h=0.1$, spread to $0.33$~mHa at $h=0.2$ (all within
$[-0.55,-0.22]$~mHa) and $0.80$~mHa at $h=0.25$, and reach a $1.66$~mHa spread at
$h=0.3$ where the best/worst registrations ($+0.86/-0.80$~mHa) just brush the
$\pm1$~mHa band. The on-site placement overshoots and the body-centered undershoots ---
a grid-quadrature aliasing that averages out only as $h\to0$. So the $1$~mHa Coulomb
tolerance holds robustly and registration-independently for $h\lesssim0.2$--$0.25$~bohr;
$h=0.3$~bohr is the practical edge, where the translation-induced variation alone
approaches the tolerance.

\paragraph*{Recommended setting.}
$\Rc=6$~bohr, $\nout=10$, $\ell_{\max}=3$: Hartree energetics converged to ${\sim}0.5$~mHa
(continuum), holding to ${<}1$~mHa on grids with $h\lesssim0.2$--$0.25$~bohr for any
registration (Fig.~\ref{fig:hartreegrid}); $N_{\varrho}=10$ monopole channels for the
spherically symmetric hole functional; and (Eq.~\eqref{eq:designrule}) scale invariance
over $\Lambda_{\max}\approx2.0$ at the coarsest $h=0.3$~bohr, rising to ${\approx}6$ at
$h=0.1$~bohr, at $320$--$960$ convolutions for the full $\ell\le3$ feature set. The
reduced gradient $s$ and the power spectrum/bispectrum are well within their flagged
tolerances at these settings (App.~\ref{app:stress}); the reduced Laplacian $q$ is
excluded on the stability grounds established there and in App.~\ref{app:gea}.
