SCF STABILIZATION of the dense-reference functional (rf001, the MAE-34 energy win). Status: DIAGNOSED;
energy-preserving LB94-gate implemented (partial); a clean self-consistent fix still open.

DIAGNOSIS (scf_vx_diagnose.py, v_x vs cached OEP exact-exchange potential, rho>1e-3):
  config                 max|v_x|    roughness(mean|d2v/dr2|)
  OEP (exact-x target)    1.7-4.9        2.5-6.1   (smooth, physical)
  backbone (ref-free)     ~1.7           ~1e6      (SCF-STABLE despite roughness)
  rf001 ridge 1e-2       30-47           ~8e7
  rf001 exact 1e-8       88-187          ~1.6e8   (huge + oscillatory)
=> the dense low-rho references ILL-CONDITION the Gram matrix; the coefficients blow up. The ENERGY is
   robust (one linear Coulomb moment, reproduced at nodes regardless of coef magnitude -> MAE 34) but the
   POTENTIAL v_x = dE/drho (full coef sensitivity) blows up to 10-100x OEP and oscillates -> SCF fails.

SCF CONVERGENCE MAP (rf001):  Be: ridge 1e-2 FAILS, 1e-1 & 1.0 converge.  Ne: 1e-2 & 1e-1 FAIL (needs
>=1.0). So a converging ridge is atom-dependent AND ruins the energy (ridge 1e-2 energy MAE 197). The
SCF does NOT diverge -- it OSCILLATES (bounded v_x, 200 iters insufficient); brute-force damping
(alpha 0.05, 600 iters) is impractically slow.

LB94-STYLE LOW-DENSITY GATE (implemented: param ref_gate_rho; gates the kernel REFERENCE deviation by a
smooth density switch, full in the bulk -> off in the no-reference tail; gates BOTH energy and potential
so v_x=dE/drho stays consistent; backbone/GEA untouched). Energy MAE vs gate (exact interp): 0(34),
0.003(32), 0.01(84), 0.03(407). => gate=0.003 PRESERVES the energy (tames the no-ref tail extrapolation
without eating the rho>=0.01 references). BUT it does NOT stabilize SCF alone (Be still fails): the v_x
is rough throughout the bulk (rho>=0.003), not just the tail.

DECOUPLING attempt (stable backbone SCF density -> exact referenced energy): MAE 78 (vs ~37 on HF) --
the backbone's LDA+FA density is too diffuse; density-driven error degrades it.

CONCLUSION: the energy and potential need DIFFERENT regularization -- the energy is robust to the
ill-conditioned coef (exact interp, MAE 34), the potential needs smoothing/accuracy. No single knob
(uniform ridge, density gate, backbone decoupling) does both. The clean fix is to DECOUPLE them:
 (a) TWO-COEFFICIENT scheme -- energy from the exact-interp coef, v_x (adjoint) from a ridge- or
     OEP-regularized coef; the SCF then converges on the referenced (not backbone) density (closer to
     HF -> smaller density-driven error than the backbone decoupling), and the accurate energy is read
     on it. Requires compute_xc to use the potential-coef for the adjoint.
 (b) OEP-anchored / potential-smoothness-penalized fit -- regularize toward the smooth physical v_x
     (OEP where cached: He, Be, Ne, Mg) rather than toward smaller coef norm.
RECOMMEND (a): it directly exploits "energy robust, potential not" and reuses the MAE-34 energy.

=== OEP FIT (oep_potential_fit.py): reveals a fundamental ENERGY-POTENTIAL TRADEOFF ===
v_x vs cached OEP exact-x potential, density-weighted RMS shape mismatch (gauge-aligned), on He/Be/Mg:
  config                          He      Be      Mg
  plain LDA+FA backbone          0.035   0.091   0.075   <- BEST potential match
  + exact-hole references        0.035   2.75    2.47    (ridge 1e-2; ALL ridges down to r=100 still
                                 0.035   0.50    0.72     WORSE than backbone)
  saturating-gradient backbone   0.035   0.45    0.53    (mu=0.22; WORSE than plain too, and grows with mu)

KEY RESULT: the plain LDA+FA backbone already has the best OEP-matching potential; EVERYTHING that
improves the exchange ENERGY -- the gradient enhancement (saturating backbone) AND the exact-hole
references -- DEGRADES the potential away from OEP. The references nail the energy MOMENT (sigma.Cmom,
one linear functional) but get the full hole SHAPE wrong, and the potential v_x=dE/drho depends on the
whole shape. So there is a fundamental ENERGY-POTENTIAL tradeoff in this hole construction.

CONSEQUENCE: "fitting OEP" does not rescue the self-consistent functional -- the backbone IS the
OEP-fit, and the references cannot be used for the potential. The best SCF-stable / decoupled result is
the backbone potential + referenced energy = MAE ~78 (does NOT beat PBE 45). The MAE-34 win is
intrinsically NON-self-consistent: it requires a hole whose potential is far from OEP.

BOTTOM LINE on SCF: the dense-reference functional cannot be made self-consistently accurate by
potential regularization (ridge / gate / OEP-fit) -- they all face the same tradeoff. To get a
self-consistent functional that beats PBE, the construction must get the hole SHAPE right (not just the
energy moment) so the references improve BOTH energy and potential -- a deeper construction change (fit
references against the potential/shape, not only the energy; or a hole representation whose derivative
is well-behaved). The MAE-34 result stands as a non-self-consistent (post-HF-density) benchmark.
