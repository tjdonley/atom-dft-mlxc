=== OEP FIT (oep_potential_fit.py): reveals a fundamental ENERGY-POTENTIAL TRADEOFF ===
v_x vs cached OEP exact-x potential, density-weighted RMS shape mismatch (gauge-aligned), on He/Be/Mg:
  config                          He      Be      Mg
  plain LDA+FA backbone          0.035   0.091   0.075   <- BEST potential match
  + exact-hole references        0.035   2.75    2.47    (ridge 1e-2; ALL ridges down to r=100 still
                                 0.035   0.50    0.72     WORSE than backbone)
  saturating-gradient backbone   0.035   0.45    0.53    (mu=0.22; WORSE than plain too, and grows with mu)

KEY RESULT: the plain LDA+FA backbone already has the best OEP-matching potential; EVERYTHING that
improves the exchange ENERGY -- the gradient enhancement (saturating backbone) AND the exact-hole
references -- DEGRADES the potential away from OEP. The references nail the energy MOMENT (sigma.Cmom,
one linear functional) but get the full hole SHAPE wrong, and the potential v_x=dE/drho depends on the
whole shape. So there is a fundamental ENERGY-POTENTIAL tradeoff in this hole construction.

CONSEQUENCE: "fitting OEP" does not rescue the self-consistent functional -- the backbone IS the
OEP-fit, and the references cannot be used for the potential. The best SCF-stable / decoupled result is
the backbone potential + referenced energy = MAE ~78 (does NOT beat PBE 45). The MAE-34 win is
intrinsically NON-self-consistent: it requires a hole whose potential is far from OEP.

BOTTOM LINE on SCF: the dense-reference functional cannot be made self-consistently accurate by
potential regularization (ridge / gate / OEP-fit) -- they all face the same tradeoff. To get a
self-consistent functional that beats PBE, the construction must get the hole SHAPE right (not just the
energy moment) so the references improve BOTH energy and potential -- a deeper construction change (fit
references against the potential/shape, not only the energy; or a hole representation whose derivative
is well-behaved). The MAE-34 result stands as a non-self-consistent (post-HF-density) benchmark.

=== REVIEW OF FORMALISM + SHAPE FIT WITH BOTH ENERGY AND POTENTIAL (corrects the "tradeoff" framing) ===
Formalism: v_x = eps + rho*d eps/d rho0 + op_n^T(ew rho * d eps/d c'_n)/ew + grad_op^T(ew rho * d eps/d g)/ew.
The reference contribution enters v_x through d eps/d(features), i.e. the FEATURE-DERIVATIVES of the
reconstructed shape sigma(x) -- and grad_op^T (a spatial derivative) AMPLIFIES roughness. So the ENERGY
depends on sigma (the shape value, a moment) while the POTENTIAL depends on d sigma/d x. The exact-interp
fit makes sigma accurate at nodes (energy) but its feature-derivatives rough (potential) -- NOT a
fundamental tradeoff, a FITTING artifact.

FIX (the right idea, per the formalism): the CONSTRAINED MIN-ROUGHNESS shape fit -- among coef matching
the reference holes exactly (Kp coef = Delta -> energy), minimize the feature-derivative roughness
coef^T G coef (G = sum_d (dK/dx_d)^T(dK/dx_d)); coef = M Kp (Kp M Kp)^-1 Delta, M=(G+eps I)^-1. Implemented
as param deriv_smooth (shape_fit_min_roughness.py prototype). RESULT (eps=100, rf001):
  energy MAE 45 -> 41 (preserved/slightly better);  OEP-potential mismatch Be 14.1 -> 6.7, Mg 7.7 -> 5.0.
=> the shape fit DOES improve BOTH energy and potential simultaneously, confirming there is no
   fundamental tradeoff (the earlier OEP "tradeoff" was an artifact of exact-interp vs ridge).

STILL OPEN (two issues): (1) the improvement is not ENOUGH for SCF -- the smoothed potential (OEP 6.7) is
still far rougher than the backbone (0.091), and SCF (deriv_smooth=100, +/- gate) does not converge.
The node-feature-derivative roughness (my G) is a proxy; the actual v_x roughness is amplified by the
SPATIAL operators (op_n^T, grad_op^T) at the eval points -- a G that penalizes the true v_x roughness
(or the OEP mismatch directly) should smooth more. (2) min-roughness smooths the GEA slope too, BREAKING
the 10/81 limit (-5.5 vs 0.123) -- a slope CONSTRAINT (linear in coef) must be added to the
constrained solve. NEXT: add the GEA-slope constraint + a v_x-targeted (or OEP-targeted) roughness penalty,
and re-test SCF. The method is right; it needs the correct roughness target and the slope constraint.
