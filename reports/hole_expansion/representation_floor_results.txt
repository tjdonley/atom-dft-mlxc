READINGS:
1. In-domain floor (~20 mHa here, sparsity-limited; ~1 mHa with dense refs) << cross-atom floor (~80).
   Transfer is the hard part -- confirmed fit-free.
2. THE DECONVOLUTION (robust): the KRR transfer errors (39750 -> 6761 mHa as we added the l=1 vector)
   sit ~6700 mHa ABOVE even this upper-bound floor (~80). So those KRR numbers are dominated by FITTING
   error; the l=1-vector "6x win" reduced FITTING error, NOT representation information. This is exactly
   the conflation the analysis was meant to expose: richer features helped the MODEL, not (visibly) the
   representation floor.
3. The k-NN cross-atom floor is ~FLAT across representations, including the full density vector V_full.

LIMITS (so we don't over-read 2-3):
- The k-NN floor is an UPPER BOUND (cross-atom neighbors are not infinitely close at 10 atoms x 80 pts,
  so it includes real variation). A flat upper bound does NOT prove the true floor is flat -- richer
  features could lower the true floor beneath it.
- The bias-correcting cross-atom Gamma extrapolation FAILED here (non-physical intercept > M=1 value):
  the gamma-delta relation is too noisy/nonlinear on sparse cross-atom data. Not usable at this size.
- Cross-representation comparison via NN is dimensionality-sensitive (V_full's 70 near-degenerate dims).

WHAT IS / ISN'T ESTABLISHED:
- Established: the KRR transfer story was largely a fitting artifact (deconvolution point 2).
- NOT established: whether richer density invariants lower the TRUE cross-atom floor, and whether V_full
  hits a density->hole (1-RDM) wall. The data is too sparse and the estimators too biased to settle it.

NEXT (to settle it): (a) scale the cache to ALL closed+open atoms and many more points so cross-atom
neighbors are genuinely close (the k-NN floor then tightens toward the true floor); (b) use a
reparametrization-invariant estimator -- KSG mutual information I(R; e_sf) and conditional
I(e_sf; V_full | R) -- which sidesteps the metric/dimensionality confound and directly answers
"does the reduction discard energy-relevant information"; (c) only then revisit the bispectrum / 1-RDM
question with a trustworthy floor.
